Low Battery Goose — Governance Roles & Application Guide

Proposed structure — not an active DAO, election, or appointment notice.

This document describes the proposed Governance Board and Community & Operations Team roles. Submitting an application does not create a board seat, voting power, token allocation, employment, compensation, or authority over project assets. Final eligibility, seat count, term lengths, selection rules, and operating procedures must be published before appointments or binding votes begin.

Welcome to the flock

The Low Battery Goose community is growing. We are inviting capable, constructive community members to express interest in helping build and support the project.

Running on 1%. Still honking.

There are two distinct groups:

  • Governance Board (roles 1–5): governance coordination, records, accountability, and community representation.
  • Community and Operations Team (roles 6–14): execution of communications, creative, community, partnership, events, education, and technical-support work.

Operational team roles do not automatically carry board membership, governance voting rights, treasury access, or authority to approve spending.

How to apply

The Governance sign-up sheet lists every role and has the application form, where you can choose up to three roles. Submissions open once the secure intake connection is configured and tested, so check that page for the current status. You may apply for a Governance Board role, an Operations Team role, or both. Share only information you are comfortable providing. Do not submit government ID, financial account details, passwords, recovery codes, seed phrases, private keys, or signing credentials. Do not submit a wallet address unless a separately published, approved process specifically requires it.

The proposed operating rules for the Board are in the Governance Board Operating Charter.

1. Governance Board

1. Governance Chair

Oversees governance activities, coordinates meetings, and ensures decisions follow established rules without having unilateral financial authority.

2. Deputy Governance Chair

Supports the chair and helps maintain continuity and accountability.

3. Governance Secretary

Documents proposals, meetings, decisions, and approvals, and publishes governance reports for the community.

4. Treasury and Accountability Representative

Monitors budgets, transaction records, and funding reports, and ensures the two-signature requirement is respected. This is an oversight and reporting role, not unilateral custody or spending authority.

5. Community Representative

Represents community concerns, gathers feedback, and helps bring member proposals to the Governance Board.

2. Community and Operations Team

6. Community and Marketing Lead

Coordinates community growth, marketing strategy, campaigns, partnerships, and overall communication.

7. Social Media and Content Manager

Handles X content, educational posts, threads, announcements, and the project's social presence.

8. Videographer and Video Editor

Creates YouTube videos, short-form content, project updates, and visual storytelling.

9. Graphics and Creative Designer

Develops memes, promotional graphics, branding, and visual materials.

10. Community Managers and Moderators

Manage Telegram discussions, welcome new members, enforce community guidelines, and protect the community from spam and impersonation.

11. Partnerships and Outreach Coordinator

Builds relationships with relevant communities, projects, creators, and potential collaborators.

12. Games and Events Coordinator

Organizes community games, contests, competitions, voting activities, and engagement campaigns.

13. Education and Research Coordinator

Helps develop educational content covering cryptocurrency, Solana, AI, blockchain security, and responsible participation.

14. Technical and Security Advisor

Reviews technical proposals, security practices, website features, and integrations, and communicates technical risks to the appropriate people.

Governance, integrity, and security commitments

Applicants should be willing to:

  • Act respectfully, transparently, and in the interests of the wider community.
  • Disclose relevant conflicts of interest and recuse themselves where appropriate.
  • Keep accurate records of proposals, discussions, decisions, approvals, and assigned actions.
  • Avoid promising token returns, guaranteed rewards, allocations, or outcomes.
  • Respect privacy and never request another person's seed phrase, private key, password, or signing access.
  • Support independent review and clear public reporting.
  • Follow the project's two-independent-signature policy for every project-controlled asset release.

Treasury safeguard: No chair, deputy, committee lead, treasury representative, developer, administrator, or individual may authorize a project-controlled release alone. Every project-controlled release, transfer, withdrawal, distribution, or disbursement requires two independent authorized signatures and a documented purpose, amount, recipient, approval record, and transaction record where available. Governance oversight does not equal custody or signing authority. This policy is not proof that a multisig is already operational; the actual wallet, signers, threshold, and technical enforcement must be verified before being described as live.

Proposed selection and onboarding process

  1. Applications are collected and reviewed against role-specific criteria.
  2. Shortlisted applicants may be contacted for a discussion or asked to provide relevant work samples.
  3. The project publishes eligibility, seat count, selection method, term length, removal/replacement process, conflicts policy, and any community ratification or voting procedure before appointments are finalized.
  4. Selected members, role descriptions, term dates, and reporting expectations are announced through official project channels.
  5. Members receive documented responsibilities, access boundaries, communication procedures, and an onboarding briefing.

No applicant is appointed merely by submitting an application. None of the following is live: token-weighted voting, an on-chain DAO, a Realm, binding governance, or compensation. Each must be approved, implemented, and verified before it is described as live.
